## Findings

No fatal mathematical gap was identified in the arguments reviewed below.
One presentation defect was found and corrected: a bare carriage return in
equation (2) of `theory/imperfect_packing_lower_bound.md` broke the TeX for
the even-m limit. It arose inside the intended `rm` command. The corrected
expression uses `text{ even}`. The formula, proof and certificate values do
not change. The supplement's source hash is regenerated by its verifier.

The fresh reproduction also exposed an execution issue: the older comparison
and switch-family negative-control scripts imported sibling modules without
adding their own directory to the import path. They worked in ordinary mode
but failed under `python -I -S`. Both now resolve their sibling modules
explicitly, matching the newer verifiers. No certificate arithmetic changes.

The following distinctions are essential: a finite witness does not prove
an analytic family theorem; numerical solver success does not prove certificate
feasibility; a valid certificate does not prove the mathematical reduction
to the stated norm; and local reconstruction does not establish novelty.

## Certificate interpretation

The comparison supplement first bounds spectral projector errors from rational
eigenpair residuals and a certified gap. Approximate orthonormal bases are
replaced by their polar factors. The Gram inverse-square-root perturbation
estimate yields the conservative polar error `42z+eta`; this feeds the effect
error budgets. The dual blocks bound the rational model's norm from above,
and primal blocks bound it from below. Summed effect errors bound the channel
error even when the rational model is not itself an exact POVM. These steps
are necessary: simply verifying the rational SDP would not prove Proposition 6.

The sharpness, reference and veto verifiers check their exact matrix data with
fractions. The audit scripts additionally use integer determinant calculations
and deliberately corrupted inputs. These independently implemented positivity
checks reduce reliance on one elimination routine, but share data and portions
of code; they are not an independent implementation of the entire proof.

## Boundary attacks

- Tiny positive `alpha`: inverses may be poorly conditioned, but exist. The
  general estimate becomes trivial where required. `alpha=0` is excluded;
  no continuity extension of the polar construction is asserted there.
- `delta` tending to zero: the formulas have the stated limiting behavior.
  The record corollary divides by `delta`, so the endpoint zero is not part
  of that statement. Threshold equality is included in each full sector.
- High ranks, degeneracies and many labels: the operator proof uses complete
  sector projections and orthogonal direct sums, not rank-one eigenvector
  perturbation. The finite rank-one certificates are examples, not evidence
  replacing this general argument.
- Zero labels: retained as zero effects; every nonzero effect must have a
  nonzero full sector. A tiny nonzero effect without such a sector is outside
  the hypotheses, not a counterexample. If `R=0`, the perfect-packing proof
  gives the stronger upper estimate `2 delta`.
- Extreme residuals and references: residual normalization is on its support,
  so zero eigenvalues are allowed. All main upper bounds are derived before
  discarding an arbitrary reference. System-only numerical searches cannot
  replace this step, as Proposition 8 explicitly demonstrates.

The decisive remaining correctness check is reconstruction by a researcher
outside this workflow. The [reviewer packet](../review/REVIEWER_PACKET.md)
identifies the proof dependencies and asks for that assessment.
